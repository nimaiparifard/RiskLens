# Fairness

Audited on the **decisions** (approve / reject at the cost-optimal threshold) of the credit
champion (v2) on the frozen test set (46,127 applications). Generated by
`risklens.fairness.report` on every training run and logged to MLflow
(`reports/credit/fairness.md` is the raw output).

**Protected attributes are not model inputs.** `CODE_GENDER` and `AGE_GROUP` are excluded
from the feature matrix (`exclude_features` in `ml/configs/credit.yaml`); the audit measures
*indirect* (proxy) disparity.

## Gender — gates promotion

| group | n | default rate | approval rate | TPR (defaulters rejected) | FPR (good customers rejected) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| F | 30,390 | 6.97% | 88.2% | 43.6% | 9.5% |
| M | 15,735 | 10.20% | 82.5% | 52.0% | 13.6% |

* Demographic-parity difference (approval rate): **0.056** → below the 0.10 gate ✅
* Equalized-odds difference: 0.084

## Age — audited, reported, does not gate

| group | n | default rate | approval rate | TPR | FPR |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| < 25 | 1,752 | 12.84% | 70.2% | 61.3% | 25.2% |
| 25–34 | 10,767 | 10.62% | 78.4% | 57.5% | 17.4% |
| 35–44 | 12,609 | 8.04% | 85.8% | 47.9% | 11.2% |
| 45–54 | 10,577 | 7.29% | 89.1% | 43.8% | 8.3% |
| 55+ | 10,422 | 5.48% | 94.7% | 24.3% | 4.2% |

* Demographic-parity difference: **0.246**; equalized-odds difference: 0.370.
* The gap follows a real difference in observed default rates (12.8% vs. 5.5%). The model
  does not see age directly, but age is predictable from employment length, document
  history and credit-history length.
* Why it does not gate (ADR 0003): under ECOA / Reg B age may be used in an empirically
  derived, demonstrably and statistically sound scoring system as long as older applicants
  are not disfavoured — here they are favoured. Other jurisdictions (e.g. the EU AI Act for
  credit scoring) may require a different policy. Switching is one config line
  (`fairness.gate_features: [CODE_GENDER, AGE_GROUP]`), and with this model it would block
  promotion.

## What mitigation would cost

Fairlearn `ThresholdOptimizer` (group-specific thresholds, demographic-parity constraint,
balanced-accuracy objective), computed on every run:

| attribute | approval gap before → after | approval rate after | balanced accuracy before → after |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gender | 0.056 → 0.000 | 71.5% | 0.682 → 0.717 |
| Age group | 0.246 → 0.002 | 67.6% | 0.682 → 0.714 |

Equalising approval rates is achievable, but the optimiser does it by **rejecting far more
applicants** (approval rate 86% → 68–72%). Balanced accuracy rises because it weights the
minority defaulter class equally, yet the business cost under our cost matrix rises
sharply. Group-specific thresholds also amount to disparate treatment, which is itself
restricted in credit. The recommended path is therefore *not* post-processing but:

1. keep protected attributes out of the inputs (done);
2. gate gender disparity at promotion (done);
3. investigate the strongest age proxies (employment length, `DAYS_ID_PUBLISH`,
   bureau-history length) with the compliance team before the next model version;
4. monitor approval rates by group in production (on the roadmap).

## Limitations
* Gender is binary in this dataset (4 `XNA` rows were too few to analyse).
* Disparity is measured on historical outcomes, which may themselves encode past lending bias
  (selection bias: rejected applicants have no observed outcome — no reject inference).
